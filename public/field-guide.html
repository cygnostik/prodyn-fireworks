LOOK A LITTLE CLOSER

A small field guide to a very big sky.

40 presets, grouped by what you see. Built from display-industry glossaries and Japanese makers’ descriptions; animation timings, colours and interface groupings are authored for Afterlight.

Some names overlap. Canopies such as kamuro and nishiki share a family; a fan is an arrangement, not a new kind of burst. Each preset’s notes keep those distinctions visible.

Blooms

Peony

Clean, coloured stars. A perfect sphere without a trailing tail.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Evenly distributed points on an expanding 3D spherical surface, not a screen-space disk.
MOTION
Rapid opening, then deceleration and modest collective sag.
TRAIL
None beyond a very short exposure-like glint; no persistent streaks.
NAMING
Kiku/chrysanthemum and botan/peony agree on the trail distinction; a tailless star can still fall.
[1][2][5][13]

Chrysanthemum

A full sphere of fine, sparkling radial trails.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Spherical array of radial heads, each leaving a visible spoke.
MOTION
Fast round opening followed by gently curving descent.
TRAIL
Fine continuous spark trails with separately aging sparks; heads remain identifiable.
NAMING
Persistent trails distinguish chrysanthemum from the largely tailless peony.
[1]

Dahlia

Fewer, brighter stars; a broad, open flower.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Sparse, large, bright tailless points with clear gaps between petals.
MOTION
Longer reach and longer readable points than the peony preset.
TRAIL
No persistent trail in the base preset.
NAMING
Larger/brighter/fewer is relative, not an absolute star count or speed.
[1][2]

Diadem

A compact, bright core sits within an open outer flower.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Outer peony plus a tight inner cluster.
MOTION
Outer sphere expands normally; core moves less and fades sooner.
TRAIL
Inherit the outer bloom; compact core stays point-like.
NAMING
An authored compact-core distinction based on HEX; other vendors use diadem much like pistil.
[3][4]

Pistil

A smaller central bloom nested inside a larger shell.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Outer flower plus one smaller concentric spherical core.
MOTION
Both layers begin together; inner layer has smaller radial reach.
TRAIL
Independent trail choices per layer.
NAMING
A core modifier presented as a ready-to-use button, not a unique particle law.
[1][4]

Double pistil

Three nested spheres opening around a shared centre.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
One outer flower plus two inner spherical cores: three visible layers in total.
MOTION
Co-centered simultaneous opening with different expansion radii.
TRAIL
Default tailless inner layers keep gaps readable; outer trail optional.
NAMING
An English preset label for two nested inner cores, not an asserted Japanese display classification.
[1][9][13]
TRY THIS EFFECT ↗

Multi pistil

An outer flower and three inner cores open as four concentric spheres.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Default one outer sphere plus three inner spherical cores; optionally four inner cores.
MOTION
Precisely concentric expansion; preserve depth and similar overall extinction timing.
TRAIL
Use restrained tails so successive cores do not merge into solid bloom.
NAMING
Three inner cores correspond to sanjushin; not three total spheres and not planar bullseye rings.
[1][9]

Canopies

Willow

Long-burning gold trails turn downward into a wide curtain.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Broad globe opening into a loose umbrella of fine hanging branches.
MOTION
Opening yields early to strong downward curvature; long descent remains visible.
TRAIL
Long, thin, relatively dim warm amber/gold sparks.
NAMING
Western willow preset; Japanese yanagi can instead be a softly falling non-spherical cluster.
[4][5][32]
TRY THIS EFFECT ↗

Brocade

A dense, glittering gold canopy with a woven texture.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Full umbrella with more densely textured hanging strands than the willow preset.
MOTION
Rounded opening into slow cascading arcs.
TRAIL
Fine gold glitter-like texture; brighter and fuller than the authored willow.
NAMING
Brocade, crown, willow and kamuro overlap; the relative density is an authored visual distinction.
[1][2]

Kamuro

A hanging crown of long, fine golden trails.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Large rounded canopy that closes into a hanging, bob-like curtain.
MOTION
Long-lived heads descend substantially after the initial expansion.
TRAIL
Long dense amber-to-silvery-gold trails with restrained twinkle.
NAMING
Modern Japanese usage often means nishiki-kamurogiku; kamuro and nishiki are overlapping names rather than universally separate types.
[4][13][15]

Nishiki

A full, sparkling golden canopy that lingers.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Same canopy topology as kamuro, with a brighter and fuller authored appearance.
MOTION
Long fall; do not replace the descent with repeated explosions.
TRAIL
Rich pale-gold/whitish sparkling trails, kept granular rather than a solid neon curtain.
NAMING
Nishiki is a preset label within the overlapping kamuro family. Hue and density are authored rather than matched to a particular maker's display.
[4][6][13][15]

Palm

A few thick branches spread above a rising trunk.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
A small number of broad fronds; optional rising trunk leads into the crown.
MOTION
Bold outward motion then curved falling fronds; visible spaces between arms.
TRAIL
Thick high-brightness trails, unlike willow filaments.
NAMING
APA describes six fronds, but this is not a universal count.

Spider

Fast, straight arms spread wide, then slow and fall.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Sparse to moderate hard radial spokes with a jagged, fast opening.
MOTION
Very fast outward travel; extinguish before substantial sag.
TRAIL
Short-lived straight bright streaks, not long drooping palm fronds.
NAMING
Flat trajectory means little visible curvature here, not a required 2D disk.

Horsetail

A soft, compact plume turns over into a close fall of parallel tails.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Compact one-sided plume rather than a full spherical flower.
MOTION
Retained upward momentum turns over quickly into a tightly grouped fall.
TRAIL
Long parallel-ish tails that remain near one another.
NAMING
Wider falling plumes are often called aerial waterfalls; keep that variant distinct from waterfall-curtain.

GEOMETRY
Points on one planar expanding circle embedded in 3D.
MOTION
Self-similar radial expansion, then modest sag and extinction.
TRAIL

Textures

Strobe

Individual stars blink independently across the burst.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Ordinary sphere of heads whose brightness is time-modulated.
MOTION
Continue head trajectories through dark phases rather than freezing or respawning them.
TRAIL
Minimal trail so on/off heads stay distinct.
NAMING
HEX suggests 1–2 Hz; any animation rate is authored. Reduced-flash mode must suppress this effect rather than being advertised as universally safe.
[1][4]
TRY THIS EFFECT ↗

Glitter

A fine succession of delayed sparkles along the trails.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
A tailed flower whose small trail sparks flash at different ages.
MOTION
Heads follow normal arcs; glitter sparklets lag behind and fade.
TRAIL
Sparse localized flashes within a granular trail, not head-wide on/off pulsing.
NAMING
A trail-domain modifier presented as a preset; loose retail twinkle terminology can blur it with strobe.
[2][6]
TRY THIS EFFECT ↗

Crackle

A burst ends in small branching flashes and sharp snaps.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
A distributed field of tiny expanding sparklets and brief microflashes.
MOTION
Follow parent stars or appear as terminal clusters; local microburst velocity is short-lived.
TRAIL
Short bushy sparks; avoid permanent glowing branches.
NAMING
Crackle, time rain and dragon eggs overlap; do not force all variants into the same timing.
[1][2][3][5][6]

Ghost

A wave of colour moves across sectors of an expanding shell.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Clean spherical bloom with a color front moving across sectors.
MOTION
Stars keep expanding continuously while their emission changes.
TRAIL
Little persistent trail, so old colors do not obscure the phase front.
NAMING
Sweeper ghost is the stronger professional description; simple vanish/reappear may be a variant but not the whole definition.
[2][4]

Colour change

Stars change colour during the same flight.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Default peony sphere with a coherent color transition.
MOTION
Continuous trajectories; color state advances with star age.
TRAIL
Default tailless; trail color and head color can be independent in other bases.
NAMING
Modifier, not a new shell topology; contrasting colors present simultaneously are not the same as a temporal change.

Salute

A short white flash and a strong report, with few stars.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
A brief compact flash with minimal persistent star structure.
MOTION
Fast flash decays into a small dim smoke cue, rather than a large colorful bloom.
TRAIL
None by default; sparse silver accents are an optional variant.
NAMING
Sound-first accent. Not all real salutes are visually identical, and no SPL or waveform was measured.

Crossette

Each travelling star splits into four smaller arms.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Primary radial stars each divide into a small four-arm cross.
MOTION
Parents travel first; daughters inherit position and some momentum, then separate.
TRAIL
Medium parent trail, short clean daughter trails.
NAMING
A plus-shaped original burst is not the defining event; the readable delayed subdivision is.
[1][2]

Falling leaves

Soft points drift downward without a hard expanding break.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
A loose cloud of persistent colored points or tiny dashes.
MOTION
Slow gravity-led descent with gentle correlated side-to-side flutter and shared wind.
TRAIL
Almost none; do not make each leaf a long comet.
NAMING
Passive drift distinguishes the authored preset from active darting fish/bees; flutter details remain artistic.
[1][3][5]

Bees

Tiny self-propelled lights dart away from the centre.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Compact swarm of erratic small lights.
MOTION
Short correlated turns and accelerations; no independent per-frame position noise.
TRAIL
Short yellow/silver spark traces; individual paths remain visible.
NAMING
Western names overlap fish/spinners; a buzz is not guaranteed by every bees product.
[2][3][5][33]

Fish

Short-lived stars swim through small curling paths.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Loose group of wriggling, independently moving lights.
MOTION
Longer graceful darts than the authored bees; motion persists rather than passive leaf settling.
TRAIL
Minimal short traces, not willow-like drapery.
NAMING
The bees/fish size hierarchy is only one vendor usage; relative scale and smoothness are authored differentiators.

Tourbillon

Spinning, spark-shedding elements swirl through the air.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Moving spinning spark sources with curled local traces.
MOTION
Combine drift with rotating emission and irregular changes of direction.
TRAIL
Short corkscrew/tumbling spray, not a static spiral-shaped arrangement.
NAMING
Accept tourbillion/tourbillions/turbillion aliases. Farfalle is related but not required as a separate button.

Shell of shells

A parent shell releases a constellation of smaller breaks.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Initial parent dispersion followed by many small spatially separated flower centers.
MOTION
Child centers drift apart before their own local spherical expansions.
TRAIL
Keep a readable gap before child blooms; each child has its own compact tail profile.
NAMING
Separate this spatial cluster from a serial multi-break effect along one continuing path; thousands is not a literal required count.

Ground

Comet

One rising star draws a thick tail; no aerial burst.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
One rising bright head with a tail; no terminal spherical explosion by default.
MOTION
Rise, decelerate, extinguish; slight arc or angled trajectory is an authored option.
TRAIL
Thick continuous tail with individually decaying sparks.
NAMING
A comet can also be an ascent accessory to another effect. Do not make the standalone preset burst automatically.
[2][5][6][32][43]

Mine

A sudden fan of stars lifts directly from the ground.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
A low broad cone or bouquet of many stars emerging together from the horizon.
MOTION
Immediate upward spread followed by deceleration and fall; no high-altitude parent burst.
TRAIL
Short clean star traces or a selected glitter/crackle texture.
NAMING
Ground-origin effect whose light moves into the air; not equivalent to a high aerial flower.
[1]
TRY THIS EFFECT ↗

Fan

A coordinated spread of angled comets across the sky.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
A spatial-temporal arrangement of multiple existing emitters or bursts.
MOTION
Arrange paths across the lower/middle sky: simultaneous spread, left/right chase, center-out, or mirrored pairs.
TRAIL
Inherited from the child effects; fan has no independent particle tail.
NAMING
Keep outside burst geometry. V/W patterns and a Z/sweep are variants; authored fan-chase spacing can start at 0.10–0.25 seconds.
[3][5][22]

Fountain

A sustained low spray of sparks and a gentle hiss.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Persistent upward spray from a fixed ground point.
MOTION
Fresh sparks rise continuously while older sparks arc outward and descend.
TRAIL
Moderate-lived granular sparks; brightness concentrated near emission.
NAMING
Gerb/fountain naming varies; this is a readable cascading fountain preset, not a hardware model.
[5][43]

Waterfall curtain

A suspended line sheds a curtain of falling sparks.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
A fixed elevated horizontal band producing a curtain of downward sparks.
MOTION
Continuous gravity-led fall from a stationary line with modest shared wind.
TRAIL
Long thin descending spark strands; no giant central aerial break.
NAMING

Roman candle

A timed succession of rising stars from one position.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Repeated individual pearls/comets from the same scene anchor.
MOTION
Each light has its own rise and extinction; optional bombette-style small terminal bloom later.
TRAIL
Inherited from pearl/comet, not a new trail species.

Wheel

A fixed ground wheel rotates, casting curved streams of sparks.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Fixed-center circular rotating pattern of spark emission.
MOTION
Spark paths leave a turning source and then fall; the wheel center stays in place.
TRAIL
Short arcs that reveal rotation without becoming a filled glowing disk.
NAMING
Do not confuse a ground wheel with a flying girandola. Angular speed is authored, not measured.
[34][43]

Set piece

A fixed outline lights up in small, steady points.

The shape of the effect
GEOMETRY
Static arrangement of colored luminous points tracing a symbol, word or drawing.
MOTION
Light holds in fixed world positions; add only tiny independent flicker.
TRAIL
Little or no moving trail.
NAMING
The button means lancework-like imagery; the broader term set piece can include other ground effects. Any lettering is original app artwork.
[1][43]
